# Planning and writing

## Select substance

For each candidate, identify the reader, point, real example, decision, source, contribution, and practical use. Keep this planning compact and internal unless requested.

Prefer choices with meaningful constraints, tradeoffs, implementation detail, or an observed outcome. Reject a generic tip when the author's source supports a deeper lesson. Never invent drama, failure, or results to make a topic interesting.

## Review available history

Use the profile's review window and authorized history. Include published posts and active drafts but distinguish them. Read the actual text when available, not only titles.

Compare the situation, reasoning, conclusion, and distinctive phrasing. Changing the hook or role name alone is not a new post. Recurring themes are allowed when the example or insight adds something useful. No universal topic cooldown or performance threshold applies.

If there is no history, say the repetition check is limited to the current batch. Missing history does not block drafting. Missing analytics does not mean poor performance.

## Plan the batch

Use the requested count or confirmed cadence. Choose distinct arguments and suitable formats. Vary the treatment because the evidence differs, not to satisfy a formula.

For each slot, retain the source, point, intended reader, closest prior post if available, and difference. If the sources cannot support the requested count, explain the gap and offer fewer substantial posts or ask for one additional source.

Use actual analytics only when supplied or accessible with authorization. Compare like periods and account for exposure differences. Reactions alone do not establish commercial value or a causal winner. Never infer reach, leads, or revenue from absent data.

## Draft

Use the smallest structure that carries the argument. Possible forms include a build explanation, decision and tradeoff, observed pattern, worked example, or project lesson. These are options, not labels to put on every post.

Lead with the substance. Explain enough of the method for the reader to use it. Use technical details when they support the point. Cut staged suspense, repeated lessons, generic advice, and unsupported claims.

Questions may be useful but are never compulsory. Do not turn each post into three questions, three steps, or the same accomplishment-advice-close sequence. Do not insert casual phrases or jokes merely to imitate a voice.

Read the draft as speech and revise unnatural phrasing. Do not claim an audio read-aloud happened unless it did. Compare with supplied writing samples when available.

## Long-form articles

Build around one larger argument. Explain the starting conditions, contribution, important choices, evidence, results, and limits. Use headings only where they help navigation. Suggest source-backed illustrations when they clarify the work. Do not fabricate screenshots, output, or performance charts.

A short post may point to an article, but it should still make sense without the click. Keep each repurposed post focused on its own argument.

## Review and learn

Before delivery, check source support, contribution, privacy, voice, repetition, and factual consistency. Give sources and material uncertainties outside the copy. If a check fails, revise the claim or flag what evidence is needed; do not certify what was not checked.

Record feedback at its actual scope. Save an agreed general preference to the working profile. Keep a one-post edit local. Track publication and metrics only from explicit evidence. Do not change the author's core subject merely because one post received fewer reactions.
